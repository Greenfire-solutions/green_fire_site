A land project is a hundred projects pretending to be one. There is the land itself, with its water, soil, slopes and history. There are the people: founders, members, investors, neighbours, the county. There is the plan, which changes every season. And there is the admin, which never stops. Most land projects that stall do not stall for lack of vision. They stall because the vision lives in one person's head and the work lives in a group chat.

Green Fire helps land projects get the whole picture out of those heads and into tools everyone can use. We call it AI land onboarding: a project goes from scattered notes and a boundary drawing to a shared map, a data file for the site, a running list of work, and an AI dashboard that carries the repeat admin.

## Who this is for

**Regenerative developers and builders** planning a new community, who need to show investors and buyers what is coming and keep crews and phases organised.

**Land trusts and conservation groups** who need a clear record of the land, its stewardship and the work done on it.

**Eco villages and intentional communities** with members coming and going and a lot of shared work to keep visible. See [eco village technology](/eco-village-technology).

**Retreat centers, farms and festival grounds** where land, events and people overlap all year.

## What AI land onboarding includes

### 1. A data dossier for the site

We start from the land. Our open source bioregional software, [BioRegional OS](/bioregional-ai), compiles public data for the site: its ecoregion, its watershed, nearby stream gauges, the soil survey, species recorded in the area, and hazard and drought data. Every layer keeps its source. That dossier is the factual base for design, permits and fundraising, and it is the starting point for a permaculture or ecological plan. See [permaculture AI](/permaculture-ai).

### 2. An interactive 3D map

People support what they can see. We build an interactive 3D map of the land, with layers for terrain, water, buildings, gardens, roads and phases that can be switched on and off. It runs in a web browser on any phone or laptop, with no app to install, and it is light enough to load at the edge of a field. Investors turn it in their hand. The county planner sees the water plan. A new member finds the kitchen. See the [interactive 3D work](/tech).

### 3. The work, on the map

Your projects and areas go into Civil X, our land project software, as tasks on the 3D map. Each task can carry a before and after photo, fingerprinted at upload so the record cannot be quietly changed later. Completed work earns credits on a chained ledger, and each project decides whether those credits are paid from a budget, counted as hours, or kept as a record. Civil X never holds money. Your own people approve and pay any work your own way. Each project gets its own copy with its own database.

### 4. An AI dashboard for the admin

Then we turn to the paperwork. We build an AI dashboard around how your project already works: drafting updates to members and investors, preparing grant and permit paperwork from the record of work, sorting volunteer and member applications, reminding people what they signed up for, and filing the documents that pile up. It prepares the work and a person approves it before anything is sent. Jordan built the first of these to run his own touring music business and runs it every day, so the hard lessons, the duplicate email, the message sent at the wrong time, have already been learned on his own account. See the [AI options](/start).

### 5. A film that tells the story

For fundraising and recruitment we make a short animated film of the land and the plan, built from your own photos and map. It gives a donor or a buyer the whole project in a couple of minutes.

## What AI does and does not do on a land project

AI is very good at the repeat work around a land project: gathering data, reading long documents, drafting letters, turning field notes into tasks, keeping records straight. It is not good at, and should never be trusted with, the decisions that make a land project what it is. It does not choose who joins. It does not negotiate with neighbours. It does not speak for the land's Indigenous history, which belongs to the people whose history it is. Our tools are built so that every one of those moments has a person at it.

We also refuse to let AI invent facts. If something was not measured, the dossier says so. A confident number from nowhere has sunk more than one land project in front of a planning board.

## Why we build it this way

Land is slow, local and specific. The technology that serves it should be too. Everything we build for land projects is local first and owned by the project: your map, your data, your copy of the software. The bioregional software is open source, so it outlives any one company, including ours. That is what we mean when we call our work [solarpunk](/solarpunk): tools that help people take care of a real place, owned by the people who live there.

## How it starts

A first conversation about the land, the people and what you want to build. Then we compile the site data and draw the first version of the map, so you can see your project in 3D before you commit to anything else. Most projects add the task software and the AI dashboard once the map is in use and the work starts to pile up.

## Common questions

### How can AI help a land project?

AI helps a land project by compiling site data, drafting the constant flow of updates, applications and reports, turning notes into tasks, and keeping records consistent. It works best preparing work for people to approve, with decisions about members, neighbours and the land kept with the community.

### What is AI land onboarding?

AI land onboarding is Green Fire's process for getting a land project organised: a data dossier for the site, an interactive 3D map, shared tasks with proof of work in Civil X, and an AI dashboard for the admin.

### Do I need to own land already?

No. Many projects start with a site under consideration. The data dossier and 3D map are often most useful before purchase, to understand the water, soil and hazards and to show partners what the project could be.

### Is my project's data private?

Yes. Each project gets its own copy of the software and its own database. Nothing about your members, photos or plans is shared with other projects, and the bioregional software runs on your own computer.
